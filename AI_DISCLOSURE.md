# Disclosure: AI assistance and authorship

**This document exists so that no reader is misled about how this work was
produced. Please read it before citing, reviewing, or building on anything here.**

---

## Summary

> **🔬 Research Artifact — AI-assisted. Unverified. Released for discussion and replication.**

This project was produced in extended collaboration with a large language model
(Anthropic's Claude). The AI wrote essentially all the code, ran all the
analysis, and drafted all the prose, including this document.

Chris Tsirkas directed the work: set the goals, supplied source material, made
the judgment calls, rejected and redirected approaches, insisted on legal and
methodological rigor at several points where the AI had been insufficiently
careful, and takes responsibility for releasing the result.

**Chris Tsirkas is not a specialist in Aegean scripts, historical linguistics,
or philology, and claims no expertise in those fields.**

He is a software architect and a playwright, and a classicist by independent
study rather than formal training — he holds no university degree in
classics, linguistics, or philology; what knowledge he has of ancient Greek
grammar, metre and literature comes from years of self-directed study, not
coursework or credentialing. His published work is in ancient Greek drama and
verse — translations and
adaptations of Euripides, original tragedies in classical and modern Greek
metre, and a 2024 book-length study, *Τραγωδία – Λόγος, Ρυθμός και Ψυχή*
("Tragedy: Word, Rhythm and Soul", ISBN 978-960-6879-79-1), examining the
aesthetic, structural and metrical anatomy of Attic tragedy, including a
dedicated section on Aristoxenus's harmonic theory and its relation to tragic
verse.

A separate project, the *Ancient Greek Verse to Music Composer*
(https://ancient-greek-music-composer.duckdns.org/ — an explicitly-labeled
technical preview, built on the metrical scholarship of Aristoxenus, Alypios
and Pseudo-Plutarch and on Marcus Mota's published research methodology),
applies computational methods to reconstructing the musical realization of
that same metrical tradition.

That is genuine engagement with the ancient Greek world, and it is **adjacent
to this subject rather than in it**: Attic drama of the fifth century BCE —
a well-attested alphabetic language with two millennia of continuous
grammatical description — is roughly a thousand years and an entire writing
system away from Bronze Age Linear A, an undeciphered syllabary with no
agreed phonetic values, and nothing in that work involves Aegean scripts,
epigraphy, palaeography or historical linguistics.

Nothing here should be read as carrying the authority of a domain expert,
because it does not.

## What this means in practice

**No part of this work has been peer-reviewed.** No specialist in Linear A has
examined it. The results may contain errors that a domain expert would catch
immediately.

**No claim of original scholarly insight is made.** The framework, the analyses
and the findings were generated by an AI system working from published sources.
Where the work is correct, the credit belongs substantially to the scholarship
it builds on and to a machine; where it is wrong, the responsibility for
publishing it is the human author's.

**The findings are unverified.** The AB21/AB22 correction, the Packard
replication, the adequacy results and the SigLA decoding are all internally
consistent and reproducible, but "reproducible" means the code produces the same
numbers again, not that the numbers are right about Linear A.

## Why it is released anyway

Three reasons, offered without any claim that they are sufficient.

The code is reproducible end to end and the reasoning is fully documented, so
the work is unusually easy to check. Everything needed to falsify it is in the
repository.

Some findings are checkable against physical evidence by anyone with access to
the GORILA plates. `TODO.md` lists exactly which plates and what to look for.
That is a concrete invitation to verification, not a rhetorical one.

The failures are reported alongside the results. Five substantive claims were
withdrawn during the work and are documented in the paper (§12.7) with what went
wrong in each case. A reader can see the error rate.

## What a specialist should be skeptical of

In roughly descending order of concern:

**The corpus provenance.** The working corpus is an unreviewed extraction of a
non-peer-reviewed tabulation of a published edition. Three sweeps against
independent witnesses substantially address this, but a specialist may not find
that sufficient.

**The Packard replication.** Reconstructed from a primary source obtained
piecemeal as page images. The method was wrong three times before it was right
(§8.2). It may still be wrong in a way the sources available did not reveal.

**The philological judgments.** Genre tagging, sign identity decisions and the
reading of GORILA conventions were made by an AI from documentation. Where
plates were directly examined (`docs/PLATE_COLLATION.md`), that examination
was held to the closest standard of human-eye visual inspection and academic
citation practice an AI can reasonably claim — not the same thing as a
trained epigrapher's judgment, and none of the 20 items reviewed cleared the
strict standard that practice set, which is reported there as an honest
result rather than smoothed over.

**The D4 finding**, which is the paper's strongest claim. It rests on a
fifteen-of-fifteen agreement between two witnesses against a third. That is
strong statistical evidence, and it is not autopsy. It should be checked against
the plates.

## A note on the SigLA data

The SigLA-dependent results use data from a publicly served file, `database.js`,
under its stated CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 license: attributed, non-commercial, share
alike. No authentication or technical protection measure was involved.

An earlier draft of this disclosure described not having asked the authors first
as a lapse. On review that was an over-correction — the license affirmatively
grants the use — and it has been replaced with this factual statement.
Correspondence with the authors has since been sent, as a courtesy and to
establish a documented route for others; see `data/README.md` item 3 and
`docs/SOURCES.md` for the current position, which holds regardless of whether
a reply is received.

It is mentioned here because the drafting error is itself an instance of what
this document exists to describe: an AI-assisted project generating confident,
plausible text that needed human judgment to correct. The correction came from
a reviewer, not from the machine.
